# Pitch Craft

Two frameworks, one flow. Positioning answers *why this, for whom, instead of what*. The
value equation finds which lever is losing the sale. Run positioning first — an offer cannot
be tightened before it is aimed.

Read Stefano's voice profile file if it exists (the `voice-profile` skill builds it) and
write all output in that voice. If the profile lists banned words, use none of them
anywhere in the output.

If `idea-gate` is available and the thing being pitched has never passed it, say so once and continue. Pitching
an unvalidated idea is polishing a guess, but the pitch is still the deliverable he asked for.

**Read before asking.** Before Part 1, read what already exists: the project's README or PRODUCT.md, the
client record, the brief, an idea-gate assessment, the conversation so far. Pre-fill every input you
can, each with its source, and show them in one block for him to confirm or correct. Then ask, one at a
time, only the inputs that are missing or vague. Asking him what the README already says wastes the turn.

## Part 1: Positioning

Ask the ones not already pre-filled, one at a time. Wait for each answer.

**Category** — What category does this live in? Name something a buyer already understands
(project management tool, brand photography, fractional CFO). Not a slogan.

**Target** — Who specifically, and in what situation? "Everyone" and "small businesses" are
not answers.

**Pain or job** — What problem are they solving, or what job are they hiring this for?

**Key benefit** — The most important result if it works.

**Alternative and difference** — What do they use today instead, and what makes this
meaningfully different from that?

### Tighten before writing

Check each answer once. Push once per vague answer, then move on — this is not a workshop.

- Category is vague if it is a slogan, not a known market.
- Target is vague if it could be almost any buyer.
- Pain is vague if it names a feature instead of a problem.
- Benefit is vague if it is only better, faster, easier, or AI-powered.
- Difference is vague if it says higher quality or unique without naming the alternative.

### The line

```
For <target>, <name> is a <category> that helps them <benefit> by <difference>.
```

One sentence. If it will not fit in one sentence, the target is too broad. Go back.

The sentence is a test of the aim, not a rigid template for the output. Do not force every
surface (a headline, a cold-email opener, a README line) into that shape; write the copy the
surface needs, once the line holds.

Then name, in one honest sentence, the weakest part of the position. Not an insult — the
part a skeptical buyer would press on first. Honest means a weakness the facts support; do
not invent one to fill the slot.

## Part 2: The offer levers

Only if there is something being sold or exchanged. Skip for a pure positioning pass.

```
Value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) / (time delay × effort required)
```

Ask one at a time:

1. **Dream outcome** — What does the customer actually want, in their words? What does their
   situation look like after this works?
2. **Perceived likelihood** — What makes a new customer believe it will work *for them*?
   Proof, cases, guarantees, credentials. Anything that reduces fear of buying badly.
3. **Time delay** — How long until the first tangible sign it is working? Not the final
   outcome. The first sign.
4. **Effort and sacrifice** — How much work does the customer put in? What must they learn,
   change, or give up?

Score each strong, medium, or weak. A lever is weak if the answer was vague, if there is no
proof, if the first result is far off, or if the customer does most of the work.

**Name the single weakest lever plainly.** Do not say all four are fine. If they genuinely
are, the offer is not the problem and the positioning is — say that instead. Name only a
weakness the answers support; do not invent one for the sake of the format.

Then give two or three **concrete** changes for that one lever. Concrete means a specific
addition: a named guarantee, a faster first step, a proof point to collect, a piece of work
moved from the customer to him. "Build more trust" is not a change.

Guarantees, prices, commercial terms and every other commitment in the output are
proposals until Stefano authorizes them. Drafting is not permission to send.

## Part 3: Deliver it

Give the result in the reply. Put it in the project's canonical record only when he asks.

- The one-line positioning, the five inputs, why the position should hold, the weakest
  part, the four levers with their assessments, the weakest lever, and the concrete changes.
- End with one next move: a headline to test, a section to rewrite, a proof point to collect,
  or a question to ask on the next call.

## Close

Print the one-line positioning and the one next move. Nothing else. When the write-up is
given in the reply, these two close it: nothing comes after them.

*Positioning inputs follow April Dunford's* Obviously Awesome; *the value equation is Alex Hormozi's*
($100M Offers).
