# Website discovery

## Core conversation

Select unanswered prompts that matter. Keep optional follow-ups in facilitator notes; do not make the client repeat supplied information or decide technical implementation.

1. **What should improve in your business because of this website?** Prioritise an outcome and how progress could be recognised. Record available baseline evidence; no invented conversion guarantees.
2. **Who are your most important customers, and what makes them choose—or hesitate to choose—you?** Needs, common questions, objections and genuine differentiators.
3. **Which offer should the website lead with?** Service, location, eligibility, pricing approach and information needed before committing.
4. **What should visitors do next, and what happens afterwards?** Enquiry or booking route, responsible person, response expectations and current friction.
5. **What should people feel about your business, and what would feel wrong?** Reference examples with reasons, desired character and identity to preserve.
6. **What credible content and assets can we use?** Service details, photography, testimonials, credentials and work examples; permissions, missing material, supplier and approval owner.
7. **What do you need to manage yourselves after launch?** Actual edits, frequency, responsible person and comfort with existing tools.
8. **What boundaries must we work within?** Budget range, recurring-cost tolerance, deadline and its reason, decision-maker, required systems and dependencies.

## Conditional branches

### No website

Ask how customers currently find and assess the business, where enquiries go and whether a domain is controlled. Review supplied profiles, listings and materials. Identify the minimum useful journey, existing evidence and content gaps. Do not assume a large multi-page site is required.

### Existing website

Ask what works, what must remain, known customer/staff frustrations and who controls editing, domain and hosting. Inspect relevant public customer journeys before the conversation when authorized and accessible. Use Impeccable where available and relevant; distinguish observed defects from hypotheses about their effect on sales.

Bring a few prioritised findings: observation → business implication → recommendation → verification. Check valuable URLs/content, search visibility, integrations and migration needs before recommending replacement. Use analytics or private source/account access only when provided or authorized. Do not submit live forms or create bookings merely to audit a site; agree a test route first. Label untested delivery and inaccessible code as unknown.

### Established brand

Identify authoritative guidelines and assets, what can change and who approves exceptions. Preserve the agreed identity. Website adaptation does not automatically include a rebrand or new logo.

### Missing or unclear brand

Explore recognisable strengths, desired impression, tone, reference examples and expressions to avoid. Translate answers into a proposed brand brief; we choose design roles rather than asking clients to pick arbitrary numbers of colours and fonts. Clarify website styling versus messaging, logo creation or a wider identity project.

### Bookings, integrations or automation

Map current tools, capacity, enquiry handling and what happens when submissions fail or nobody responds. Identify minimum necessary information, sensitive-data implications and maintenance ownership. Collect an account/access inventory without passwords; arrange minimum necessary access later. Technical validation and security checks remain our responsibility.

## Turn answers into a brief

Summarise confirmed answers rather than reproducing the questionnaire. Include only relevant fields:

- Priority business outcome, audience, leading offer and differentiators.
- Main customer journey, action and operational destination.
- Proposed pages/features, exclusions and unresolved decisions.
- Approved evidence/assets; missing content, owners and dependencies.
- Brand direction: impression, voice, messaging, retained identity, proposed typography, colour roles, imagery, layout and motion; reference reasons and approval owner.
- Client editing tasks, systems, account ownership and recurring costs.
- Budget/deadline constraints, approver and next milestone.
- Acceptance: observable completion of the main customer task, client ability to perform agreed updates and appropriate measurement.

Label design recommendations and scope as proposed until approved. Missing information may remain an explicit open decision with an owner; do not fill it with invented answers.

## Close and hand off

Use 1–3–1 if choices are ready: one priority problem, up to three genuine approaches and one recommendation with a verification step. Confirm priorities, material needed, owners and the next milestone.

Before commercial commitment, settle scope, feedback/revision rounds, delayed-input dependencies, ownership/licensing, one-off and recurring costs, change approval and support duration/coverage. Do not bury all of these in the first discovery questionnaire. Ask the material questions now; carry remaining terms into scope agreement.

Design reviews, device/accessibility checks, code review, production verification, recovery planning and handover follow the building process. They are delivery obligations, not questions asking the client whether basic quality is wanted.
